# More ideas

Status, 6 November 2026: this is the archived 1 November feature inventory. The current app opens in D — Workspace; Insights keeps the earlier charts. Command-Shift-N now opens Quick capture. See [Workspace release](workspace-release.md) for current behavior, design references and integration limits.

These are in the app. Home opens on the counts and the bar charts. Notes are bullets, separate from tasks. Tab indents a bullet. Shift-Tab pulls it back. Due dates offer EOD, Tomorrow, In 2 Days, EOW, and Next Week.

Two limits stay. The Mac Dock cannot grow a menu from this app, so that list lives in the menu bar. The desktop card is a small window, not a Mac widget. Command-Shift-N logs a meeting. Command-Shift-Y says what is due. Spotlight can find the file named Due today in Documents.

Read on 1 November 2026 from the products named at the end. The other apps were not opened. A few pages did not return their article text. Those are listed under "Could not read."

## The meeting note

1. Keep a day you will do the work, separate from the day they need it. Home still follows the day they need it.
2. Hide a follow-up until a day you choose, without moving the day they need it.
3. Keep a catch pile for something you heard before you know who asked. One quiet line on home only while the pile has something in it.
4. Keep a someday shelf that stays off home until you open it on purpose.
5. Split one person's long list into named sections, such as hire or budget.
6. Void a promise so it leaves the list and does not count as finished work.
7. On home, show only the next follow-up for a person. The rest wait on their page.
8. Pause a person so their open tasks leave home until you unpause them.
9. Turn one line of the meeting note into a task, and later open that same line from the task.
10. Say the follow-ups out loud and have them land in the meeting sheet, on this Mac.
11. Keep topics to talk about, separate from tasks, and tick a topic when you have talked about it.
12. When you log the next meeting with the same person, offer the topics you never ticked.
13. Keep a private box on the meeting. It stays off the brief and off anything you save out of the app.
14. Drop a topic on a person between meetings, so the next sheet already has it.
15. Split the meeting note into named topics, each with its own short lines.
16. Under a decision, write why you chose it and what you turned down.
17. Mark each task as yours to do or theirs to do. Who asked is already kept. This is who must do it.
18. Keep a list of questions the meeting did not answer, and show them on the brief before you meet again.
19. Write the next meeting's date and what it should cover. This is separate from the usual check-in rhythm.
20. If they ask for the same thing again, fold the new note into the old task instead of making a second card.
21. Fold extra lines under one line in the note, and open them only when you want them.
22. If you write someone's name in a note, show that meeting on their page too.
23. Pin the meeting you are still in at the top of that person's page.
24. Let a meeting or a task cover a stretch of days, such as a visit, not only one day.
25. Next to an open meeting, show who else you wrote about on that same day.
26. On a person's page, mark the days that already have a meeting note.
27. Open one date and see every meeting and every ask from that day.
28. Keep a link on the meeting back to the mail or the page it came from.
29. Drop a mail onto their page. The subject becomes the task. The body stays as the note.

## Time

30. Keep an optional clock time on a task. Home stays grouped by day.
31. While you type, drag the "read as due" line onto another day before you save.
32. Tap a day on the week to start a meeting already set to that day.
33. Nudge at a clock time you pick, not only on the morning of the due day.
34. Choose the hour when a day-only follow-up nudges you, and when it starts to count as late.
35. Follow their city's clock, so five in the evening means five in their town.
36. Mark how long a follow-up needs, such as half an hour. Review can add up today's time.
37. After you save a meeting on a day the calendar already shows as full, offer one short leftover task.
38. If a day's calendar titles say travel or offsite, do not offer that day first.
39. Start a clock while you do the work. After a few finishes, show how long this kind of task usually takes.
40. If a follow-up would come back on a weekend or late at night, hold it until the next morning you work.
41. Bring a follow-up back the next time you open this Mac, rather than at a clock time.
42. Hide a follow-up until the next time you log a meeting with that person.
43. When a hidden follow-up comes back, keep it in a short pile that disappears when the pile is empty.
44. Mark an away stretch. Late tasks stay on their page, and home and the check-in nags stay quiet until you are back.
45. Nudge when you arrive at or leave a place. The place stays on this Mac.
46. Nudge when you start a message with that person, so the open ask is there before you type.

## The person

47. Keep the names of the people in their life, so you can greet them next time.
48. Keep dates that belong to them, such as a birthday. These are not check-ins, and they stay off home.
49. Keep one line for how you met. Later notes do not replace it.
50. Keep a dated note to ask how something went. It is not a work task and not a check-in.
51. Keep a short list of gifts you have given and gifts you might give.
52. Star a few people and gather them in one quiet list off home.
53. Group people from one event, such as a conference, separate from a company.
54. Mark a promise day on a person, such as their board or their trip, on the calendar you already have.

## Order of the work

55. Mark that one follow-up waits until another is done. Review says when you can start.
56. If the later one is due before the first can be finished, mark those two days.
57. If you will see them soon, raise their open tasks even when the due day is later.
58. Keep a count on a task, such as three of five, so a part done still shows.
59. While you finish one task, jot a new ask for the same person without leaving that task.
60. Work only that one person's open tasks until you stop.
61. Open a four-box look on purpose: matters and soon, matters and can wait, soon but light, and neither.
62. Set how much follow-up time you want in a week. Review compares this week with that amount.

## The shape of the day

63. Open a short screen when you are done for the day.
64. On that screen, write one line for the thing worth remembering.
65. After the morning pick, write one line for what may get in the way.
66. At the end of the day, choose which leftovers move to tomorrow. The rest stay where they are.
67. Once a week, name a few aims for the week ahead. The recap you already have looks back. This one looks forward.
68. Open one follow-up in a small window and hide the rest while you do it.
69. Paste a copied line and save it as a follow-up for the last person you logged.

## Outside the window

70. Put a small card on the desktop with what is late and due today. Tick one done without opening the home screen.
71. Add Shortcuts actions to log a meeting and to hear what is due today.
72. Find a person, or what is due, from Spotlight, and open only that.
73. Click the menu-bar icon and get today's names, then Log a meeting. The count you already have stays.
74. Control-click the app in the Dock and get the same short list.

## A quieter screen

These change how the current screens behave. They are not new places to store a task.

75. Keep the evening tasks in a faint strip at the bottom of home, out of the way of the day.
76. Show the date control only when the pointer is on that row.
77. Fold a home group, such as Later or No date, down to its title and count.
78. Keep the row you are on dark, and fade the other rows.
79. Open Log a meeting as a blank note. The person, the date, and the tasks appear when you leave the note.
80. While the meeting sheet is open, keep the keyboard inside that sheet.
81. Follow the Mac setting that reduces motion. Jumps fade instead of sliding.
82. Give every date chip a spoken name, and say whether it is selected.
83. Write the word Late next to the red. The current paper, text, green, and red already clear the usual contrast bar for small text (about 15 to 1, 5.7 to 1, and 6.6 to 1). Add a stronger setting for when the Mac asks for more contrast.
84. Tab through the main fields of the meeting sheet. Use the arrow keys inside the date chips.

## Where the ideas came from

- Things. A start day is when you begin. A deadline is when you must finish. The inbox is a temporary place, and a task with no plan yet stays out of the active list. https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/2803579/ and https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/4001304/
- OmniFocus. A defer day hides future work. A planned day is when you intend to do it, and it is not the due day. A person on hold leaves the active list. https://support.omnigroup.com/documentation/omnifocus/universal/4.10.1/en/outline/ and https://support.omnigroup.com/documentation/omnifocus/universal/4.10.1/en/perspectives/
- Todoist. A task can carry a duration. https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/features/set-a-task-duration-L1kYkZv8d
- Fantastical. While you create something, you can drag the preview to another day. A sentence with no clock time becomes a whole-day item. https://flexibits.com/fantastical/help/adding-events-and-tasks
- Sunsama. Weekly planning is for aims, and the daily tasks wait until each morning. https://help.sunsama.com/docs/usage-guides/weekly-objectives/weekly-planning/
- Akiflow. Copy a line and turn it into a task. https://akiflow.com/features/tasks-capture
- Reclaim. A task can carry a duration, and it does not have to become a calendar event. https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/16558552-tasks-overview-decide-what-to-work-on-when
- Fellow. A private note on a meeting is visible only to you. Talking points are for the discussion and do not take a due date. https://help.fellow.ai/en/articles/4274961-private-notes and https://help.fellow.ai/en/articles/4105406-action-items-v-talking-points-v-bullets
- Granola. A decision should keep why it was chosen. https://www.granola.ai/blog/client-meeting-notes-template
- Linear. You can mark something blocked, blocking, related, or a duplicate. New items in triage stay out of the normal lists until you accept them. https://linear.app/docs/issue-property and https://linear.app/docs/triage
- Monica. A person page can hold the names of the people in their life. https://www.monicahq.com/en/features/
- TickTick. The four boxes are another way to look at the same tasks. They are not a second copy. https://help.ticktick.com/articles/7055782055577124864
- Microsoft To Do. The day list resets, and unfinished items come back as suggestions you can accept. https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/todo/my-day-and-suggestions
- Agenda. A note can carry a range of dates. https://agenda.com/manual/en/dates-events-and-reminders/
- Craft. A task stays tied to the writing it came from. https://support.craft.do/en/plan-and-do/tasks
- Obsidian. An unlinked mention is a place where the name appears even though nobody made a link. https://github.com/obsidianmd/obsidian-docs/blob/master/en/Plugins/Backlinks.md
- Apple Reminders. After a time, you can pick a city for the clock. You can also be reminded when you message a person. https://support.apple.com/guide/reminders/add-or-change-reminders-remndc729e28/mac
- Apple widgets. On a Mac, a widget can sit on the desktop and in Notification Center, and the Reminders widget can mark a task done. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets.md
- Apple menu bar and Dock. A menu-bar extra should open a menu. A Dock menu appears when you secondary-click the icon. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/the-menu-bar.md and https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dock-menus.md
- Apple accessibility. Small text should clear 4.5 to 1. Do not use color as the only signal. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility.md
- Bear. A fold mark appears at the left of a section. https://bear.app/faq/how-to-fold-sections-in-bear/
- iA Writer. Focus mode keeps the current sentence clear and fades the rest. https://ia.net/writer
- Superhuman. You can send an item back to the moment you are at the computer again. https://new.superhuman.com/remind-me-29124
